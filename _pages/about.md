## Work in Progress

Han, Yongjie, *Divide the Empire*. Work in progress.<br>
Draft available upon request.<br>
[Slides](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/ojg5dzxh7xb7zul0h3y7o/Divide_the_Empire_Nov7.pdf?rlkey=mjunsvvn0rb5w4989yg50iy9f&st=v00ltdbl&e=1&dl=0)

<details class="paper-abstract">
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>How would autocrats "gerrymander" their territories? I model the formation of subnational administrative boundaries under a rent-maximizing Leviathan that designs a tessellation to maximize its payoff given the threat of secession posed by local governors. Since stronger provinces can facilitate rent extraction but also increase the risk of secession, geographic inefficiency can be politically desirable to the ruler insofar as it constrains local power. I investigate the underlying geometric implications using a quantitative simulation with granular information on the historical geography of China. I find that, once political considerations are accounted for, (i) boundaries become less compact and more interlocking than under the efficient benchmark, (ii) their boundaries follow geographic barriers less closely, and (iii) these distortions diminish as state capacity increases. In this view, inefficient internal borders are the spatial form of the control--efficiency trade-off in autocracies.</p>
</details>

## Resting Papers

Han, Yongjie, *Too Hot to Commit? Extreme Heat, Developer Output, and Collaboration Networks in Open-Source Software* (September 14, 2026). Available at SSRN: [https://ssrn.com/abstract=7582038](https://ssrn.com/abstract=7582038) or [http://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7582038](http://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7582038)

<details class="paper-abstract">
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>I examine the direct and indirect impacts of extreme temperature on digital, high-skill labor in the context of open-source software using a large, high-frequency panel of 739,587 active GitHub users linked to local weather. Exploiting within-user variation in daily maximum temperature, I first document that extreme heat significantly reduces individual output, measured by commits, and find little evidence of recovery in subsequent days. However, among geographically distant collaborating user pairs, the decrease in one user's commits is associated with an increase in the other's. These findings suggest that heat imposes a measurable cost on digital high-skill work but one that greater diversification of workplaces and collaboration networks may help mitigate.</p>
</details>
