Han, Yongjie, *Too Hot to Commit? Extreme Heat, Developer Output, and Collaboration Networks in Open-Source Software* (September 14, 2026). Available at SSRN: [https://ssrn.com/abstract=7582038](https://ssrn.com/abstract=7582038) or [http://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7582038](http://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7582038)

<details class="paper-abstract">
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>I examine the direct and indirect impacts of extreme temperature on digital, high-skill labor in the context of open-source software using a large, high-frequency panel of 739,587 active GitHub users linked to local weather. Exploiting within-user variation in daily maximum temperature, I first document that extreme heat significantly reduces individual output, measured by commits, and find little evidence of recovery in subsequent days. However, among geographically distant collaborating user pairs, the decrease in one user's commits is associated with an increase in the other's. These findings suggest that heat imposes a measurable cost on digital high-skill work but one that greater diversification of workplaces and collaboration networks may help mitigate.</p>
</details>
